# Editorial Guide

## Voice

Listening Notes should read like a contemporary music magazine: informed,
concise, and occasionally opinionated without becoming academic or corporate.
The visual language is dark, restrained, typographic, and closer to liner notes
than a dashboard. Photography earns its place through editorial value.

## The origin of RADAR

RADAR began with an unfinished search. During a Tokyo trip, a visit to Jazz
House ALFIE in Roppongi and a search begun with a cousin nearly led to seeing
Tsuyoshi Yamamoto live. Missing that show left a loose thread: a reason to
return to Tokyo, return to ALFIE, and keep looking for music.

Yamamoto is the current guide, not the destination or the project’s supreme
musical authority. The search now reaches jazz, virtuosos, improvisers, bass
players and instrumental innovators, Latin music, progressive and heavy music,
rock, orchestral music, and anything else that makes us stop and listen. Seeing
Yamamoto is a goal; after it happens, the listening continues wherever it leads.

## Selection rule

An event appears because a specific artist, setting, repertoire, lineup,
historical moment, cultural exchange, listening-history connection, geography,
or live reputation makes it unusually compelling. Absence is editorial
judgment, not an accidental gap to be filled. Genre labels describe music; they
never decide whether it belongs. Jazz is a major gravitational center, not a
boundary.

Do not mechanically prioritize every jazz event or every appearance by a
favorite artist. Exceptional musicianship, improvisation, instrumental
innovation, a living master, a Japanese-jazz connection, Latin identity,
cross-genre work, a meaningful venue or DMV connection, and continuity with
the archive are signals—not a rigid scoring algorithm.

## Bilingual practice

English and Spanish are equal editorial surfaces. Keep one shared factual event
record, then write language-specific editorial fields under `editorial.en` and
`editorial.es`. Translate meaning, rhythm, and cultural register—not words.

Spanish should feel natural in Latin American/Mexican usage. Avoid bureaucratic
or literal phrasing; for example, prefer language about making space for a show
or that a concert merits the trip, rather than constructions about spending
money to attend one.

## Factual versus editorial

Factual fields include date, time, venue, lineup, URLs, prices, and sources.
They must be traceable. Editorial fields include priority, why it matters,
travel verdict, listening suggestions, and post-show reflections. They may be
subjective, but should be specific and dated when their context can change.
